### 3. "How I Run 14 SaaS Products With AI Agents" (Jakub / @jakubinit)
https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-i-run-14-saas-products-with-ai-agents-one-month-report-49075e9757

The "propose, don't execute" model is smart. I run automated social media agents for my SaaS and learned that lesson the hard way — giving agents full autonomy sounds efficient until they post something tone-deaf at 3am.

Your point about over-automation is important. There's a sweet spot where agents handle the repetitive parts (publishing, monitoring, triage) but humans make the judgment calls. I ended up building a "doctor" module that diagnoses agent failures and attempts self-healing before escalating to me. Curious about your API costs running 14 products. At what point did the agent infra cost more than the manual time it saved?

### 4. "SaaS built in 10 days, 3 weeks live, zero paying customers" (manishbhusal)
https://www.indiehackers.com/post/ [buscar URL exacta en IH]

Respect for sharing this openly. Most posts here are success stories — the "zero paying customers" posts are more useful because that's where 90% of us actually are.

A few things that worked for me (also zero revenue for the first months):
- Free tier with real value, not a crippled trial. People need to use the product enough to feel the upgrade pain.
- Being active in the communities where your users already hang out. Not dropping links — actually helping people. The product sells itself when your profile says what you built.
- Pricing lower than you think you should. I went with 9.99 EUR/mo when competitors charge 39-49 USD. It removes the "let me think about it" barrier.

What's the product? Happy to give specific feedback if you share.

The faceless YouTube automation numbers are interesting but I'd push back slightly — the quality gap between AI-generated and human-created YouTube content is still massive.
